# British English

Use this file only when the user chose British English. The final text must be in British English, whatever spelling the source or the writer's sample uses.

- **Spelling:** -ise and -isation (organise, realise), -our (colour, behaviour), -re (centre, theatre), -ogue (catalogue, dialogue), -yse (analyse), -ence (defence, offence), and a doubled l before a suffix (travelling, cancelled, totalling), but fulfil, enrol, and skilful. Also grey, tyre, kerb, storey, sceptical, artefact, ageing, cheque (a payment), and programme for a broadcast, a schedule, or a plan of work. Software is a program in British English too, so an app, a tool, or an analytics program stays program. Use judgement, but judgment for a court's decision.
- **Do not over-correct:** capsize, prize, seize, and size keep their z.
- **Noun and verb pairs:** licence and license, practice and practise, advice and advise.
- **Words:** got, not gotten; towards, afterwards, and backwards; autumn, holiday, flat, rubbish, CV, and maths for fall, vacation, apartment, trash, résumé, and math; Monday to Friday, not Monday through Friday. Swap a word only when the British word means the same thing in context. Use whilst and amongst only if the writer does.
- **Grammar:** A collective noun may take a plural verb where that sounds natural ("the team are").
- **Punctuation:** A full stop or comma goes inside closing quotation marks only when it belongs to the quoted words. Mr, Mrs, Ms, Dr, and St take no full stop. Keep the writer's choice of single or double quotation marks and of the serial comma.
- **Dates and numbers:** 21 July 2026, not July 21, 2026. Write per cent, not percent, when the word is spelt out.
- **Leave alone:** quotations, titles, proper names (World Health Organization, Pearl Harbor), code, identifiers, commands, paths, and URLs.
