# American English

Use this file only when the user chose American English. The final text must be in American English, whatever spelling the source or the writer's sample uses.

- **Spelling:** -ize and -ization (organize, realize), -or (color, behavior), -er (center, theater), -yze (analyze), -ense (defense, offense), and a single l before a suffix (traveling, canceled, totaling), but fulfill, enroll, and skillful. Also gray, tire, curb, story (a floor of a building), skeptical, artifact, aging, check (a payment), program in every sense, and judgment in every sense. Use catalog, and use dialogue for speech but dialog for a software dialog box.
- **Do not over-correct:** advertise, advise, compromise, exercise, revise, supervise, surprise, and televise keep their s, as do other words that end in -ise in both varieties, such as promise, precise, and expertise.
- **Noun and verb pairs:** license and practice are both noun and verb. Advice is the noun and advise the verb, as in British English.
- **Words:** gotten is standard ("has gotten worse"); toward, afterward, and backward, without the s; fall, vacation, apartment, trash, résumé, and math for autumn, holiday, flat, rubbish, CV, and maths; Monday through Friday. Swap a word only when the American word means the same thing in context, and keep a word Americans also use naturally, such as autumn. Use while and among, not whilst and amongst.
- **Grammar:** A collective noun takes a singular verb ("the team is", "the government has"). Use learned and spelled rather than learnt and spelt.
- **Punctuation:** Periods and commas go inside closing quotation marks, even when they are not part of the quoted words. Colons and semicolons go outside. Mr., Mrs., Ms., Dr., and St. take a period. Use double quotation marks, with single marks for a quote inside a quote. Keep the writer's choice of the serial comma.
- **Dates and numbers:** July 21, 2026, not 21 July 2026, with a comma after the year when the sentence continues. Write percent, not per cent.
- **Leave alone:** quotations, titles, proper names (Ministry of Defence, Labour Party), code, identifiers, commands, paths, and URLs.
